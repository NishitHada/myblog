Money is an abstract concept, it doesn't exist in real life. Resources, on the other hand, are tangible & finite. Money is just a means to exchange resources.

Once AI has taken over the world, it will reduce the value of resources. What will remain valuable in that case? For that, we need ask ourselves what makes something valuable? I think value of anything isn't absolute, rather it's assigned by society at large. Also if something is free & readily available, it can't be valuable, no matter how necessary it is. We can take the case of water or air for that matter- absolutely critical for human existence but readily available hence not valuable. On the other hand, gold is totally optional, yet it is considered valuable. It has to do with gold being inert not loosing it's value over time.

But on a higher level, I think value is derived from finiteness, & because human existence is finite, anything that requires a human will be valuable. Same can be applied for things requiring effort & intuition/creativity.

Therefore, in a post-scarcity world, only things created any of human time, effort & creativity will be valuable, rest everything will be free.
